## Abstract

Coral reefs are among the world's most diverse ecosystems, providing critical ecosystem services including storm protection, maintenance of biodiversity, and productive fisheries. The reefs surrounding Mo'orea, French Polynesia are a globally significant research site representative of broader Indo-Pacific coral reef systems that are impacted by numerous anthropogenic stressors and natural disturbances that vary in intensity at sites around the island. Historically, the north shore’s outer reef has frequently experienced severe disturbance events, making it a focal area for long-term monitoring. Prior surveys have shown that within a few years, coral cover previously returned to pre-disturbance levels; however, a severe marine heatwave in 2019 caused massive coral mortality. Nearly 7 years later, the expected recovery has not yet materialized.

This capstone project identified demographic bottlenecks, the population-level constraints slowing coral recovery on the north shore’s outer reef, and quantified coral population demographic rates at eight sites across two reef habitats around Mo'orea. These findings will allow the MCR LTER to understand how different coral communities are structured, assess how they responded to the 2019 heatwave, and predict future changes in coral species composition and abundance, informing the design of management interventions aimed at supporting reef recovery.

This is a capstone project for the [Master of Environmental Data Science](https://bren.ucsb.edu/masters-programs/master-environmental-data-science) at the [Bren School of Environmental Science and Management](https://bren.ucsb.edu), University of California, Santa Barbara.
